# beginning notes

i think the best way to play this game is to do as much as you can without following a walkthrough. there's a way to get hints in game, and you can ask people you know who have played the game for help, but if you really can't figure something out, here's where you should look.

**that being said,** there's some really cool stuff that's super easy to miss, and that the fandom will be talking about all the time. this is where i really encourage getting some outside help to point you in the right direction and make sure you get that optional content

# get the game

if you're on a Windows or Linux computer of some kind, the game is available both on [itch.io](https://armor-games-studios.itch.io/in-stars-and-time) and [steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1677310/In_Stars_And_Time/). on console, you can get it on the [Switch](https://www.nintendo.com/us/store/products/in-stars-and-time-switch/) and [PS4/PS5](https://store.playstation.com/en-us/concept/10007419/). if you're on a Steam Deck that's a linux computer.

## what about the content warnings?

with a game like this you know there's gonna be something a little Concerning. so yes, there are content warnings! these are paraphrased from the game's website.

you can click the warnings to find out more, with very mild spoilers for themes of the game's content.
for more warnings, you can check [doesthedogdie.com](https://www.doesthedogdie.com/media/1069678).

> [!warning]- mental illness and trauma
> the timeloops take their toll on Siffrin's mental health, and they become more depressed as time goes on, with some mania mixed in. there are also trauma responses and other behaviors associated with bad mental health.

> [!warning]- death (temporary)
> loops in time are generally triggered by death. these deaths aren't shown visually (always some sort of cut to black). they are shown through text, sometimes in some detail, but nothing super graphic. they are also shown through audio composed of videogame-y 8bit-like sounds. emotions around the death are also depicted through text.

> [!warning]- violence
> the story is set in a world in which hostile monsters exist that the party must fight, which is a gameplay mechanic. the antagonist is a boss the party must defeat and may fail to several times. references to past violent events are also made, such as Siffrin having lost an eye.

> [!warning]- self-harm and self-sabotage
> as time goes on, Siffrin becomes more self-sabotaging. he starts taking actions that harm himself, occasionally physically and deliberately. they also sacrifice themself at times, or express wanting to do so.

> [!warning]- suicide
> with death being the main mechanism of looping, there come times where Siffrin gets stuck and cannot progress, and must kill themself in order to loop back. this is usually done non-violently by freezing themself in time.
> however, later in the game, it is possible to obtain the option of Siffrin killing himself through more direct means. it is repeatedly discouraged and warned for by characters in the game, and there are consequences for following through with it anyway.

> [!warning]- unreality
> there are times where Siffrin encounters hallucinations and struggles with their ability to perceive reality.

> [!warning]- children in active danger
> one of the party's members is a preteen, and is in active danger at points in the story. there's a point where they are threatened and physically restrained. it is implied that they're killed offscreen, but not permanently, as they are "revived" by time looping back.

> [!warning]- dubious consent
> in an optional scene, one character kisses another without knowing if they would consent. they are pushed away, but the reason is left ambiguous.

### what content warnings are there NOT?

> [!info] sexual content
> the "dubious consent" warning from above is about something romantic, not sexual.

> [!info] gore
> while things happen that would be gorey, none of it is shown or described in extreme detail.

> [!info] permanent death
> no character in the story dies permanently, by nature of the time loop. the closest there is to permanent death is the villain getting permanently frozen in time.

# pre-game

alright, let's start! first thing you're gonna wanna do is boot up the game. well first you're gonna wanna buy it and install it really. you done that? okay cool!

let's go to settings, first. you can navigate the menu with the arrow keys and enter. explore this for a bit. if you go to Keyboard Configuration, you can change the game to use WASD instead of the arrow keys. you can also connect a controller and use that if you want!

there are some visual settings too, but those unfortunately aren't accessible on the title screen. once you're done, let's start a new game!

# ACT 1

## introduction

when you start the game, you'll be greeted with a cutscene and some dialogue. once you get control of your character, go north and talk to mirabelle. your choices in this conversation don't affect anything, but you only get this one chance, so have fun with it!
### the menu

you can skip this section if you want, but i've bolded the parts that i think you should check out regardless.

press escape to go into the menu, and take a look around! you'll see your party and their stats, and a couple submenus.

in your Pockets, you'll find all your items in four different categories. items in the Pockets category are things you can use in battle, mostly healing items. any of them that aren't greyed out can be used outside of battle too - just press enter on the Pockets menu button and press enter to use the selected item! Equipment is your weapons and "armor", and Memories are some memories, which are also equippable. in the Souvenirs tab you'll find all the little trinkets you've picked up along the way. you can interact with these two! **you've got a Silver Coin to start with - try interacting with it!**

in the Craft menu, you'll find all your party members' Craft skills and their descriptions. Craft is like magic. you can't use any of these outside of battle now, but you'll be able to use some later!

in the Equip menu, you can see all your party members' stats and items they have equipped. you'll eventually get new equipment and memories you can swap out later for different stats.

**check the Profile section!** here you'll see all your party members' profiles, including their name, pronouns, Craft type, their stats, and a little description about them. get familiar with these faces, because you'll be seeing them a lot! you're Siffrin. look at that little guy. so full of whimsy. i'm sure that won't change.

if you open up your Options again now, you can access the Visual settings, which might help if you're sensitive to movement. the background scrolls in battles, which you can disable here, and there are also some screen flashes that you can disable too.

## getting ready

now go explore! explore everything! go on!!! if you ever get stuck, you can check your reminder note in your pockets for a reminder on where to go.

but even so, this *is* a walkthrough, isn't it? alright, fineeee. *i* say you should first go around town and go into every building can and talk to everyone ever, but you don't *need* to do that. but it's fun.

you *do* need to do some things, though. first, go straight up ahead to the Change God statue and interact with it. you'll get a little exposition cutscene! after that, go find your party members and ask them all the questions. Isabeau will be last.

once you've finished talking to Isabeau, go to the Favor Tree and interact with it. your choice here doesn't matter a ton, it only affects a few lines of dialogue. choose whichever you like!

## the House

the game mostly leads itself from here. just head through the halls and the story will guide you through it. you'll go through a few battle tutorials. you *could* skip past some of the Sadnesses, but you shouldn't!

when you get to Death Corridor, just check all the pillars and report back to your party.

progress through the dialogue some more, and hey, look, you got a cool new Memory! go ahead and check it out, equip it if you like! you probably should, it's pretty good. +5HP per loop, huh?

...what *is* that voice you've been hearing this whole time?

anyway, let's go talk to mirabelle. and all your friends for that matter. you'll fumble your way through it a bit, but it's fine, you just went back in time, that's normal!

after you've talked to all your friends, go stop by the Favor Tree, like that... weird voice told you to. you'll meet someone there! go talk to them.

Loop will teach you about a fun fast-forward mechanic. as they say, you can use this for conversations you've heard before, but things probably won't change much. they might a little bit! but if anything really changes, you'll snap out of it.

so, you've got a friend now, it seems. Loop will be your guide of sorts, but also a companion back in Dormont. you can talk to them about your sidequests and your main quest whenever you get stuck. if you want more immersion, it's better to rely on them than it is to rely on me! Loop won't have all the answers, but they're at least a conversation partner to bounce ideas off of. if you can't think of something, maybe they will. just a note, though - sometimes the quest help questions will be greyed out as if you've asked them before, but that doesn't mean Loop will say the same thing. they'll probably say different things at different points in a quest. ask multiple times!

once per loop, you can also talk to Loop about something random, which you should definitely do! there's some fun conversations to be had there, and if you like the game, you'll probably regret missing out on it if you don't take a look at them.
